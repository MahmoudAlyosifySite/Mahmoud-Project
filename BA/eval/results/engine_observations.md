Every figure quoted here is in sections 1-6 or in the named results file.

**What changed between raw VoxCPM2 and BA.** BA uses the same weights, seeds, texts and voices. The differences are: reference-plus-transcript cloning with an automatic Whisper transcript, the reference trimmed to its speech and loudness-normalised, digits and symbols written as Egyptian/English/French words, Latin loanwords in Egyptian text written in Arabic script, and loudness-normalised, watermarked output. On the held-out Egyptian main set, CER drops from 0.069 to 0.030, similarity rises from 0.713 to 0.769 and the Egyptian dialect share from 0.819 to 0.889. The similarity and dialect gains match what Phase 1 saw for VoxCPM2 with the gold FLEURS transcript (0.771 / 0.903 on the same utterances), so the automatic transcript costs little. There is no per-component ablation, except the code-switch and chunk experiments, so the gains cannot be split between components.

**Digits.** On the held-out digit sentences CER falls from 0.122 to 0.073 and dialect share rises from 0.667 to 0.833 (n=18). Whisper writes most spoken numbers back as digits, so this CER is still the better of the spelled and the digit reference; it measures whether the right number was said, not whether it was said the Egyptian way. The normaliser reproduces all eight hand-written Egyptian references exactly (tests).

**English.** Raw VoxCPM2 fails G3 English on similarity (0.655 < 0.699) and produced 2 empty outputs; BA passes (0.779) with none. This is the largest practical change: English was not listable on the raw base.

**Code-switching.** The plain CER (Latin reference) is slightly worse for BA (0.284 vs 0.274) because BA's Arabic-script loanwords change what Whisper writes. With both scripts mapped to one form, BA is better (0.074 vs 0.084) and the dialect share rises from 0.722 to 0.944 (n=18). The policy was chosen on new tuning sentences, but the lexicon was written knowing the evaluation words, which are among the most common loanwords anyway.

**Chunk length.** Applied as written, the rule chose 1000 characters, so held-out passages are generated in one piece. The rule ignored similarity, which in the tuning experiment fell from 0.88 (150 characters) to 0.83 (1000), with only 2 passages per setting (chunk_length.json). The held-out passages are only 2 per language, so neither choice is settled; the planner may want to amend the rule.

**Reference thresholds (deviation).** The pre-written rule on the Phase 1 SNR proxy gave 36.2 dB, which would reject about half of the real clean calibration clips. The adopted gate uses a 1st-percentile noise floor: it rejects every reference with SNR <= 15 dB pink noise and no real clean clip, and it only warns at 20 dB, where similarity already drops by about 0.09 (reference_thresholds.json, "adopted").

**Margins.** Cross-language French similarity is 0.696, exactly 0.80 of the Egyptian ceiling (0.870); this is outside G3 as written but would be at the bar if cross-language were included. French rests on 3 French voices, and Egyptian on 6 FLEURS voices whose clips are read MSA, not colloquial speech.

**Limits of the evidence.** All quality numbers are automatic (Whisper large-v3, ECAPA, one dialect classifier, DNSMOS trained on English); no one has listened to the BA outputs yet. Timings are on the A40; ZeroGPU runs without torch.compile on different hardware, so the no-optimize rows are the closer guide, and the Space was tested only on a plain GPU. The reference ASR (whisper-large-v3-turbo) matches large-v3 on colloquial Egyptian (0.070 vs 0.071) but is worse on NileTTS (0.034 vs 0.019).
