\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

For Egyptian Arabic voice cloning, the measures a practitioner would reach for first, intelligibility, speaker
similarity and predicted quality, do not see the dialect. Used to choose a base among the licence-eligible open
models, each of them picks a model that fails a dialect bar calibrated on real colloquial speech in most resamples
of texts and voices, and our own pre-specified rule did exactly that. A rule that applies the dialect bar first
selects a different base, on which \BA, an inference-time engine for Egyptian text, references and long inputs,
reaches the intelligibility of the lowest-CER system while keeping the dialect, with no new training. For any dialect,
we recommend that evaluations report a dialect measure calibrated on real dialectal speech next to error rates, that
selection rules state the dialect bar before the first model is run, and that native listeners check the decision:
in our blind test they heard the lowest-CER model as far less Egyptian, exactly as the classifiers did.
