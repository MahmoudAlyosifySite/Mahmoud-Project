\section{Listening test: design, analysis and deviations from the protocol}\label{app:listening}
The protocol (\texttt{BA/paper/human\_eval/listening\_test\_protocol.md}) was fixed before any rating was collected.

\paragraph{Design}
Four systems (\BA, \vox, \cbthree, NAMAA) and real speech, on held-out sentences never used for tuning. Each of 12
counterbalanced lists has two practice clips (not analysed), 24 generated clips on 24 different sentences (six per
system; sentence $k$ of list $l$ is spoken by system $(k+l) \bmod 4$, and the voice rotates with $l$), four real
colloquial clips from the calibration set as a hidden anchor, two repeats of generated clips, and four
speaker-similarity pairs (one per system, on sentences outside the list, against a second real clip of the cloned
speaker). Every clip is trimmed of leading and trailing silence and normalised to $-23$\,LUFS. For each clip the
listener rates how Egyptian it sounds (1, not Egyptian at all, to 5, a native Egyptian speaker) and how natural it is
(1, clearly machine, to 5, completely natural), and says whether a word was wrong, missing or garbled; for each pair,
whether the speaker is the same (1 to 5). Names are hidden and the order is random per listener.

\paragraph{Listeners and exclusions}
Adults who grew up in Egypt and speak Egyptian Arabic natively, recruited through the authors' personal networks; the
page asks for age band, gender, governorate and listening device, and no name. Of the sessions started,
\R{mlt:unfinished} were not finished and one was the first author's own test of the page; both are excluded. The
quality checks of the protocol were made concrete for this design before any system score was computed: the
real-speech check passes when at least two of the four real clips are rated 4 or more on both scales, the consistency
check when at least one of the two repeats is rated within one point of its first rating on both scales, and a
listener failing both is excluded (\R{mlt:excl} of \R{mlt:complete}). The \R{mlt:n} remaining listeners come from
\R{mlt:govs} governorates and completed \R{mlt:lists} of the 12 lists.

\paragraph{Analysis}
As pre-specified: cumulative-link mixed models (logit link, R package \texttt{ordinal}) of each rating on the system
with random intercepts for listener, voice and sentence; the contrasts \BA $-$ \vox, \BA $-$ \cbthree and \vox $-$
\cbthree on both scales, Holm-corrected over the six; mean scores with two-way (listener and sentence) bootstrap
intervals; Krippendorff's $\alpha$ with the ordinal metric; and the rank correlation of the per-system Egyptian rating
with both classifiers and with CER. Script: \texttt{BA/eval/scripts/listening\_analysis.py}; aggregate results:
\texttt{BA/eval/results/listening\_multi.json}. Individual answers and the audio stay private, as the consent text
promises.

\paragraph{Deviations from the protocol}
(i) The cloned voices are the six FLEURS speakers of the held-out set (three women, three men), so the listeners
rated the clips on which the
automatic measures of \cref{sec:dissociation,sec:results} were computed; the protocol planned recordings of six to ten
consenting volunteers, which were not collected. (ii) The design above replaces the protocol's sketch of about 60 clips
(four repeats, two real clips) with 36 ratings per listener, to keep a session near 12 minutes. (iii) The protocol asked
for headphones; the page also accepted earbuds and loudspeakers, and the results by device are reported in
\cref{sec:dissociation}. (iv) Listening stopped at 20 complete sessions of listeners not involved in the project, the
upper end of the planned 12--20; lists were assigned in turn to every session started, so the lists of unfinished
sessions stayed empty.
