Title: Impossible or Merely Improbable? A Three-Way Test of Physical Surprise in Latent World Models
Authors: Mahmoud Alyosify
Suggested primary category: cs.LG (Machine Learning)
Suggested cross-lists: cs.AI, cs.CV
Comments: 18 pages, 2 figures. Code, pre-registered protocol and results ledger: https://github.com/MahmoudAlyosifySite/Mahmoud-Project
Abstract (1558 of 1920 characters allowed):
World models are tested for physical understanding by their surprise: an impossible event should raise their prediction error. A rare but lawful event raises it too. We add that missing control: a benchmark of possible, improbable and impossible billiards clips in four families, where the improbable clips are as unusual as the impossible ones but break no law. On ideal latents, every reading of prediction error ranks lawful fast clips above clips whose energy jumps (AUROC 0.03 for the largest error, 0.42 for the best reading). The largest error of a pixel JEPA's own predictor, in a model we trained, does the same (0.06); its best reading reaches 0.64. A label-free score assembled from known parts (slow features at long lag, read between bounces and collisions) separates impossible from improbable clips on ideal latents where a conserved quantity is broken (AUROC 1.00 in the energy family and 1.00 in the stochastic family) and is blind to a teleport, which conserves everything. It is fragile: per-frame latent noise of 0.003 standard deviations removes the energy invariant. On the pixel JEPA's own latents, whose linear read-out of the frame stays far below our pre-registered gate, it finds no conserved quantity at all, and two label-free measures place that latent where the method already fails on synthetic noise. All results come from one working session and await an independent rerun. The benchmark, the protocol fixed before the runs, the code and the ledger of every run are at https://github.com/MahmoudAlyosifySite/Mahmoud-Project.
