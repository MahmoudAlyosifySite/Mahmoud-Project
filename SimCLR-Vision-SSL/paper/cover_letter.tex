\begin{letter}{The Editor-in-Chief\\\emph{Neural Networks}}
\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit the manuscript \emph{``Where Do Scarce Labels Belong? Evaluating Contrastive Pretraining under Equal
Compute and Realistic Label Budgets''} by Mahmoud Alyosify, Mirna Imbabi, Natalie Nashed and Hazem M.\ Abbas for consideration as a
research article in \emph{Neural Networks}.

When labels are scarce and unlabeled images are plentiful, should the few labels go inside a supervised
contrastive loss, or be kept until after label-free pretraining? A fair answer needs equal compute for every
method and an evaluation that uses only the labels that exist; the standard all-label linear probe does not. We
satisfy both with a compute-matched $2\times2$ design that varies the use of labels and the pretraining pool independently, on
CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10 and Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 encoders. Labels inside the loss buy
speed, not a better end point: with 10\% of the CIFAR-10 labels, SupCon leads SimCLR by 13.3 points after 970
steps and trails it by 6.6 points after 97,000. The all-label linear probe overstates encoders that use labels in
pretraining: after 9,700 steps it puts a semi-supervised SupCon 1.9 points ahead of SimCLR, whereas fine-tuning on
the 5,000 available labels puts it 3.9 points behind with the default recipe and only level with the best recipe
for each encoder; holding out labels for recipe selection does not close the gap. A seen/unseen measurement of
class tightness is consistent with this. All claims rest on three to five seeds with a
hierarchical bootstrap, Holm correction and equivalence tests, and the code, job files and per-run results
are public.

We believe the paper fits the scope of \emph{Neural Networks} on learning systems and deep learning: it
concerns how representation learning objectives use supervision, and how their evaluation can change
conclusions about them.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration by another journal. A
preprint will be posted on arXiv (cs.LG). All authors have approved the submission and declare no competing
interests. The use of generative AI tools in preparing the manuscript is declared in the manuscript, as the
journal requires.

\closing{Sincerely,}
